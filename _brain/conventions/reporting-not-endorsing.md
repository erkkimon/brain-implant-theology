# Reporting, not endorsing

Decided by Tom on 2026-09-24, founding the theology implant
([journal](../journals/2026-09-24.md)).

## The rule

**We claim that somebody said something. We prove that. We do not claim that
what they said is true.**

What they said may be true or false — this repository takes no position and
does not need one. What it asserts is narrow and checkable: that a named
person, council or confession held a stated position, in a citable text, at a
known date.

That is a *stronger* epistemic position than advocacy, not a weaker one.
"Calvin taught double predestination, here is the passage in the *Institutes*"
is verifiable and either true or false. "Calvin was right about double
predestination" is neither — it is a position, and positions belong to readers.

## Why the rule is load-bearing here

This repository holds Orthodox, Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed, Pentecostal,
Adventist and Episcopal material on the same contested questions. Those
traditions genuinely disagree, often about matters their adherents consider
salvific. A repository that adjudicated between them would be one tradition's
repository wearing a neutral name, and every other tradition would be right to
distrust it.

Reporting rather than endorsing is what lets them coexist without the
repository becoming an argument. It is the wall the building rests on, not a
diplomatic softening.

## In practice

1. **Attribute, never assert.** Not "baptism regenerates" but "the Lutheran
   confessions hold that baptism regenerates (*Large Catechism* IV.74)". The
   subject of the sentence is the person or tradition, not the doctrine.
2. **Present the disagreement, not a winner.** A contested doctrine page lists
   the positions taken on it, each with its texts, and stops there. The point
   of divergence — the precise verse or premise where traditions part — is
   usually the most informative thing on the page, so name it exactly.
3. **Criticism lands on the argument.** A tradition's objection to another is
   reported as *that tradition's objection*, cited, in its own words where
   possible. Never as the repository's verdict.
4. **Proportion follows the sources, not the editor.** A position held by one
   tradition for four centuries and one held by a single writer for a decade
   are not given equal space merely to appear balanced.
5. **No weasel neutrality.** "Some believe" hides who. Name them, cite them,
   date them — [every claim carries its receipt](every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md).

## The boundary with apologetics

The same rule holds in the sibling implant, and the split between the two is
defined by **who the opponent is**:

| | Opponent | Implant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defending Christian belief against non-Christian views | outside the faith | apologetics |
| Defending one Christian view against other Christian views | inside the faith | theology |

The distinction is not about subject matter. The resurrection appears in both:
arguing for its historicity against a sceptic is apologetics; arguing about
what it accomplished, between traditions that all affirm it, is theology. Ask
who is being answered.

The Bible is shared. Both implants cite it; neither owns it. Where a passage is
argued over *between Christians*, the argument lives here.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean the repository has no standards. A claim still needs its
citation, evidence still outranks authority, and a misattributed quotation is
simply an error to be fixed. Neutrality governs *what we assert*, not *how
carefully we assert it* — the care is governed by
[every claim carries its receipt](every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md) and
[evidence before authority](evidence-before-authority.md), and it is strict.
