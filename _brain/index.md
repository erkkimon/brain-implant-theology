# Theology implant

A brainpick brain implant covering **theology**: subjects that help Christians
defend their Christian views against *other Christian views*. Defending the
faith against non-Christian views is apologetics and lives in the sibling
implant.

Everything here is written in the neutral point of view. A page proves that a
named theologian, council or tradition *said* something. Whether what they
said is true is not this repository's claim to make.

Read `conventions/` first — the rules constrain what every other read is for —
then `skills/`, then `knowledge/`.

## conventions

- [Addressing scripture](conventions/addressing-scripture.md) — A verse is addressed by an atomic reference token — jhn14v6 — carried in the chapter document's frontmatter and in the verse's own heading, because brainpick's BM25 tokenizer splits John 14:6 into john/14/6 and cannot then tell it apart from John 6:14; the token is the retrieval key that makes finding content by verse work at all, and it is written by the ingest tool, never by hand.
- [Credence is a model, not a measurement](conventions/credence-is-a-model-not-a-measurement.md) — Numeric probabilities attached to hypotheses in this implant are the output of a stated, replayable model — a decomposition into factors, a prior, and explicit likelihood ratios with their justifications — never a claim to have measured a truth; the contribution is the structure and its defensible inputs, so any reader can substitute their own numbers and recompute, and the spread of results across reasonable inputs is itself the report on how much of a belief is evidence and how much is faith.
- [Every claim carries its receipt](conventions/every-claim-carries-its-receipt.md) — No statement of fact enters this implant without a resolvable, verifiable citation attached to that specific statement — a DOI, a mission dataset, a pinned document revision — because the value this implant adds over a model's own memory is not the fact but the source, and a fact whose source cannot be checked is indistinguishable from a fluent guess.
- [Evidence before authority](conventions/evidence-before-authority.md) — A claim is weighed by the evidence behind it and never by who made it — a famous apologist's argument and an unknown blogger's paper are graded on the same scale, criticism lands on the argument rather than the person, and a claim's authorship is a separate question (whose claim is this, provably) from its truth (does the evidence hold).
- [Reporting, not endorsing](conventions/reporting-not-endorsing.md) — The neutrality rule that lets every Christian tradition share one repository: a page proves that a named theologian, council or confession said something — that claim is checkable and either true or false — and never asserts that what they said is correct; this also fixes the boundary with the apologetics implant, where the opponent is outside the faith rather than inside it.
- [Written to be public](conventions/written-to-be-public.md) — This implant is written for release to the open internet — every page is addressed to a stranger with no access to the machine it was written on, so no host names, internal paths, personal details or credentials ever enter it; the repository is private only until it is published, which changes nothing about how it is written.
