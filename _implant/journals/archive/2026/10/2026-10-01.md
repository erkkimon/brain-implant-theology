# 2026-10-01

## The Hebrew text is in, through a versification map

- **WLC is stored** as the `WLC` section of all 929 Old Testament chapters, under the KJV-numbered tokens. The source is in Hebrew versification, which differs from the KJV in 138 chapters (Gen 31–32, Exod 7–8, nearly sixty Psalms because their titles count as verses, Joel 2–4, Mal 3–4 …), so it cannot go straight through [Add a Bible translation](../../../../skills/add-bible-translation.md).
- **New tool, `skills/tools/versification_remap.py`.** It re-numbers a Hebrew-versified source into KJV numbering using the standard `eng` mapping of the [Copenhagen Alliance versification specification](https://github.com/Copenhagen-Alliance/versification-specification) (CC BY-SA 4.0, attributed in `raw/translations/wlc/LICENCE.md`), and refuses to finish unless every Hebrew verse is used once, the word count is unchanged, and the chapter and verse counts equal the KJV file's.
- **Edge cases the map does not settle, handled and reported rather than guessed.** Psalms 51, 52, 54 and 60 have a two-verse Hebrew title; the first Hebrew verse is joined to the next token. Num 25:19 opens KJV Num 26:1. Four English verses are halves of one Hebrew verse (Isa 64:1, Ps 13:6, Neh 7:68), and they keep the KJV and Finnish text but have no Hebrew section of their own, because splitting a Hebrew verse is a scholarly call, not a mechanical one.
- Ingest is idempotent: a second run wrote 0 chapters.

## Westcott–Hort is in

- **Source cleared first.** Robinson's electronic text ([byztxt/greektext-westcott-hort](https://github.com/byztxt/greektext-westcott-hort), "Public Domain. Copy freely.") is stored at a pinned commit with its README. CrossWire's WHNU (CC BY-NC-SA) and CCEL's copy (unclear licence, garbled text) were rejected; reasons are in `raw/translations/wh/LICENCE.md`.
- **New tool, `skills/tools/wh_convert.py`.** The source is Greek in Latin letters, so it transliterates to the unaccented form `TR` uses, and stops on any unmapped character or any verse the KJV numbering lacks. Sanity check: the result averages 94% word-sequence similarity with `TR`, and John 14:6 shows the known W-H reading without the article.
- **The text itself had apparatus in it.** Robinson's `[c:v]` versification markers inside verses were stripped (14), and the `| alla | all |` alternative-reading marker in Mark 1:45 was resolved explicitly. Both are reported by the tool, not silently handled.
- **Double brackets.** W-H's `[[ ... ]]` (passages the editors judge later additions) were being read as wiki-links and created 13 ghosts; the converter now writes them `⟦ ⟧`, and the implant compiles with 0 ghosts.
- **17 verses W-H omits** (Matt 12:47, Mark 9:44, Acts 8:37, John 5:4, Rom 16:24 and others) have empty text in the source, so their tokens have no `WH` section. That is a real textual difference between the editions, now visible in the chapter files.
- 260 NT chapters now carry `WH`; a second ingest changed nothing. The spine's text step ([plan](../../../../plans/mvp-then-iteration.md)) is done.

## Mary Magdalene, the first doctrine page

- Tom asked for a Mary Magdalene article, possibly before the Phase 4 gate. [The page](../../../../knowledge/doctrines/mary-magdalene.md) is a doctrine page rather than a biography: it quotes the gospel verses it rests on (each as a verse token), and sets out the one question traditions answer differently — whether the Luke 7 sinner, Mary of Bethany and the Magdalene are one woman, two or three.
- Sources are the 1910 *Catholic Encyclopedia*, the Holy See's 2016 decree press release, and a pinned Wikipedia revision. Gregory's Homily 33, the Orthodox position and the Fathers are reported at second hand and marked so; fetching them from primary editions is open on the page and in the [to-do list](../../../../todo/open.md).
